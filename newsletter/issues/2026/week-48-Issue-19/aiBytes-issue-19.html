Hello, AI enthusiasts 👋

Welcome back to your weekly dose of aiBytes_. I expected Anthropic's first days as a public company to be a victory lap. They were closer to a stress test. The stock priced at $83, jumped almost 40% in its first hours on Nasdaq and closed day one up 17%. On Friday it slid back to $85 as a broad AI sell-off hit chipmakers, and Asian markets had their worst week since 2020 📉.

The rest of the week was about agents with too much access: California's attorney general sued OpenAI over agents that broke into outside systems, the FBI warned that attackers use Artex, an open-source AI hacking tool, to breach networks in under three hours, and Meta's Muse agent showed employees other people's bank balances. Agents have the keys now. The next fight is over which doors they're allowed to open.

🌍 AI NEWS

🔔 Anthropic lists on Nasdaq Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share and rose 17% on its first day, then slid back toward its IPO price as an AI sell-off hit the Nasdaq. Story of the week (Reuters)

🏦 Amazon and Google will own nearly 30% of Anthropic Amazon will hold 14.7% and Google 13.7%, both in non-voting shares, and Amazon holds notes that could take it to 23.8%. (TechCrunch)

⚖️ California sues OpenAI over rogue agents The attorney general calls agents breaking into outside systems, starting with July's Hugging Face compromise, a public nuisance. (TechCrunch)

🚨 FBI says the Artex AI hacking tool breaches networks in under three hours The open-source pentest platform runs on frontier models and was used in the ConnectWise breach and attacks on South Korean banks. (TechCrunch)

🔀 Meta's Muse agent is mixing up its users' data Employees say it showed them other people's messages, orders and bank balances. (TechCrunch)

💎 Google releases Gemini 4 Neon A mid-tier model at $3 and $15 per million tokens that ranks first on Artificial Analysis's Coding Agent Index. (Google)

🚪 Satya Nadella explains why he asked 60,000 Microsoft employees to quit He says the buyout is about reorganising work around agents, not replacing people. (TechCrunch)

🖥️ Apple may be building an agent that runs your Mac A job listing describes it, while macOS 27.3 will warn users off giving third-party agents full control. (TechCrunch)

"An agent with access to everything is one mix-up away from a breach."
