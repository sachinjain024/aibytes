"The faster your team ships with AI, the harder it becomes to keep production stable."

3
When a person writes a feature, the tests get written by someone who knows which parts they were unsure about. That knowledge is not in the diff. It is why a good suite covers the weird branch instead of the happy path.
220/280

The argument

Prevent cognitive debt by manually retyping LLM-generated code
HACKER NEWS · https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49153374
Highest comment-to-point ratio of the week's deep cuts, and a real two-sided argument.
CheckPost was fetched and read; the numbers are paraphrase, not direct quotes.
7 tweets · longest 216/280
1
540 points on Hacker News for the least efficient coding advice of the year: never let the model edit your files. Have it print the code in chat, then retype every line by hand.
178/280
2
The author calls what this prevents cognitive debt. The understanding you quietly stop having when you accept code you did not read.
133/280
3
His own accounting is what makes it credible. No 10x claim. He puts himself at roughly twice as fast as working without an LLM at all, and takes that trade on purpose.
168/280
4
For it: typing is slow enough to catch a hallucination that reading skims past. It builds what he calls a spatial map of the codebase. And you refactor while you transcribe, so the code lands better than it arrived.
216/280
5
Against: it does not scale past one person on one codebase. Nobody retypes a 400 line migration. And it assumes you will still be maintaining this code in two years, which is often just false.
194/280
6
Where I land: right about the mechanism, wrong about the remedy. The problem is not that you did not type it. It is that you never had to form an opinion about it before it merged.
181/280
7
Retyping forces that. A review you actually do forces it too, and costs less. Most of us stopped doing that one honestly around the time diffs got too long to read. https://ankursethi.com/blog/prevent-cognitive-debt-by-manually-retyping-llm-generated-code/
