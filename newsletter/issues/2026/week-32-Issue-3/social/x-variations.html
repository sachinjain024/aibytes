The teardown

diegosouzapw/OmniRoute
GITHUB · https://github.com/diegosouzapw/OmniRoute
Fastest riser of the week and a design worth explaining, not just a star count.
6 tweets · longest 226/280
1
OmniRoute picked up 7,141 stars last week. 38,127 total. One MIT gateway, 290+ providers, 500+ models, one endpoint. Sits behind Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Cline, Copilot. Worth understanding why it is spiking now.
217/280
2
The feature doing the work is quota aware auto fallback. A provider rate limits you or a free tier runs dry, it reroutes the call instead of failing it. Around 90 of the supported providers have free tiers.
208/280
3
Why now: model choice stopped being a one time decision. Three price moves in a single week. If a vendor SDK is wired into your call sites, every price move is a refactor. A gateway is a seam. Swap the model, leave the code.
226/280
4
The cost, which no README leads with: Every prompt takes one more hop through a service you do not run. Longer data path, wider outage surface. And fallback can quietly answer from a weaker model than the one you evaluated.
225/280
5
Fine for a chat window. Not fine for an eval, a batch job, or anything you bill for.
84/280
6
Where I land: Local dev and side projects, yes, today. Production, only with the routing decision logged per request. The day quality drops you need to know which model actually answered. https://github.com/diegosouzapw/OmniRoute

Prelint (Product Hunt #1 of the week, 640 upvotes)
PRODUCT HUNT · https://www.producthunt.com/products/prelint
Sharpest thesis of the launches, and the failure mode is about to be everywhere.
CheckFirst person is about the failure mode, not about using Prelint.
6 tweets · longest 247/280
1
The #1 launch on Product Hunt last week is a bet on one failure: your AI writes correct code that is still the wrong code. Prelint, 640 upvotes. It reviews every PR against your ADRs, docs, and past decisions. Not bugs. Drift.
228/280
2
The shape of that failure: An agent ships a clean, tested, well described change that reintroduces a pattern your team walked away from two years ago, for a reason nobody wrote down anywhere the agent could read.
213/280
3
Every reviewer who knew the reason is busy or gone. The linter has no opinion. CI is green. It merges. You meet the reason again later, in an incident channel.
160/280
4
Their number: on teams already running several AI reviewers, roughly 40% of issues fixed before merge come from Prelint. Vendor numbers are vendor numbers. But directionally it says the other reviewers were all reading the same layer of the diff.
247/280
5
The catch is the dependency. Any of this only works if your decisions are written down somewhere a machine can read. Most ADR folders I have opened are three entries deep and last touched in 2023.
197/280
6
The tool is downstream of the discipline. If you have been meaning to write the decisions down, the agent in your pipeline just handed you a deadline. https://www.producthunt.com/products/prelint

The argument

AI companies are shredding rare books (797 pts, 513 comments)
HACKER NEWS · https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49068738
Highest heat per point of the week, and a genuine two-sided argument.
CheckThe primary source is a tweet thread. Read it before posting.
6 tweets · longest 253/280
1
797 points and 513 comments on Hacker News for six words: AI companies are shredding rare books. The practice is destructive scanning. Cut the spine off, feed the pages flat, the book is gone.
193/280
2
It is not new. Libraries have done it for decades with damaged stock and duplicates. What changed is the buyer and the volume.
127/280
3
For it: a rare book nobody can read does less good than a scan everybody can. Physical scarcity is not a virtue on its own. And it is legal. You can do what you like with a book you paid for.
192/280
4
Against it: the destruction is permanent, the benefit is private. The scan goes into a training corpus, not a public archive. Weights are not readable, citable, or lendable. Nobody checks the copy against the original later, because there is no later.
253/280
5
What makes it land harder than it would have a year ago is the rest of the week. Top labs are publishing less research. Frontier work is closing. Now the inputs are being consumed the same way the outputs are being closed off.
228/280
6
No clean side here, and I am suspicious of anyone who says there is. But if you are training on acquired material, saying where it came from costs you nothing. https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49068738
